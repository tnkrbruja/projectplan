# projectplan
This will have the purpose and essentially will end up serving as the read me file

I've been told that I can only do 3 hobbies ta time, because when I take one on, there is a lot that goes with it. Between the time, the creativity, the PURCHASES, limited space, etc in a family of 3, we don't have much room to accommodate that. Well, we do, but it's not organized. 

When I got into crochet, I didn't know a thing about it and had overpaid so dang much for that Woobles yar.

In a year, I went from not knowing how to even hold the hook, almost got killed with frustration making a magic circle and learned how to do a double crochet over a week to making super intricate pieces of art. 

When this happens, you end up with a lot of stuff. Like a lot. A few bucks here, a few bucks there. No biggie. Next thing you know, you are BURIED in yarn, plastic eyes, polyfil, very expensive crochet hooks, and by the time you realized that apps like Ravelry, Stitch counter and the like just weren't going to cut it.

Things got rocky in my professional life, so I couldn't take the time to crochet that I used to. I had the discipline to process my yarn pretty quickly, then I realized I didn't know what it was after a few weeks. I didn't know the fiber type, how much I used, etc. My spreadsheet was very tedious. And I literally have a whole wall full of really good quality yar. Very frustrating. I must have made the same Yarn_log spreadsheet half a dozen times. 

Then, after fighting for my job after being threatened with termination because of cripling anxiety when we Amazon returned to office ("RTO"), I got laid off. That was a whole other story. I took time to craft and love and got into Cricut, I found myself in the same snowball I found myself in when I took on crochet. I alos really loved the mechanics of tinkering and creating really neat things. 

I honestly didn't know if I was made to be an engineer anymore, which sucked because it paid really well, and at the height of it, I was called a "wizard" and a "magician". I had the chops for it, but there was just something that felt so wrong about going back to a field that chewed me up and spit me out. I don't think I've ever sucked at life that hard before. Then the kicker. I had the chance to secure a really cool job, with a really cool company. I came in confident and was like: "SQL is my coding test? PLEASE. I got this." I had also just turned 40. Things were looking really good for the first time in a while. But I choked. COMPLETELY BOMBED. I couldn't even think about what a ratio was or how it was calculated.  I couldn't believe it. I can't honestly remember the last time I didn't get what I aimed for (like I said, I was able to appeal my old boss' decision to try to fire me, IN FRONT OF A TEAM OF RANDOMLY SELECTED ENGINEERING MANAGERS, ENGINEERS AND JUST REGULAR OLD AMAZONIANS. AND I WON!" 

It was very hard. 40. Not sure if I can cut it doing what I used to love, and unemployment was paying me less than a quarter of what I used to make with less than a month left in its payout. I got depressed. Really, really, really depressed and scared. Crafting and creating was something that kept me focused and engaged during the days. This couldn't happen anymore. I couldn't and can't let that be how my daughter sees me handle failure.

I had to sit there and really think about what it is that I love to do, what drove me to learn to code when my daughter was a toddler, and face the reality that I wasn't being true to myself. I had to create a different plan, and I needed to do it quuickly, and I needed to be genuine about it. When I was let go, every piece of work I had built over the past almost 15 years was taken. I spent months trying to dig through the documents that I managed to have printed from the week prior and just really dug into trying to pull my worth from this stack of papers. 

As I took a good look at what my value was, I knew a few things. 1. Amazon is an amazon and pretty tenacious company to move up through the ranks in. 2. I loved the crafting and tinkering from that experience. You see, when people hear I worked at Amazon, they think of the retail website with lots of crap. Very few people knew that they also had their own indie and enterprise print on demand company that was based in Charleston. I worked with graphic designers, understood submission specifications, understood the manufacturing floor and had a blast doing so. The inputs and outputs, making people's lives better through creating and manufacturing, the graphic design component of it. It was really neat. That time can and will not go ot waste.

I realized that combining my data engineering talent, understanding how important QR codes are, wanting to do the fun crafting stuff but still work on my engineering artifact portfolio, then stumbling upon JennerMaker's crafting inventory binder, it all clicked. 

I would use this all to move forward and leverage QR codes (like Amaozon does in manfufacturing anf fulfillment) to track, categorize, plan, showcase and continue to tinker. I would take all that is me and start from stratch, relearn everything, set aside my distate for AI (sorry, Gemini) and make soemthing that I can use in my engineering portfolio, be intuitive and still get to make stuff. 

At first it was going to be dubbed: "Andrea's creation station". Eh. It wasn't a bad name necessarily, but it was cheesy and didn't have pizzaz. Then I went back to my time at Amazon, when I was really trying to get buyin to work on a project called: "Project Tinkerbell" and how that's what I've been doing all this time. Tinkering and adjusting and growing and how I just wanted to feel like a wizard and magician again. But there was no way I'd get away with calling soething I was going to show to a lot of people: "Project Tinkerbell". If it went big, I'd be done by Disney. Then I remembered when George and I asked the Alexa what a female magician is. The resposonse was along the lines of a witch, a temptress, soceress (pretty negative if you ask me). I'm Mexican, so I was like: "how about Tinker Bruja (meaning witch) instead?".

It took me a good month to get the courage to see if Tinker Bruja was even a thing. Turns out it wasn't. Things are feeling good again. After some hiccups, I'm here, with my own site, a love-hate relationship with AI (I do try to limit my use of it) and hope you understand the labor of love this thing is. It's for the tinerker in us all and is fun, becasuse you get to scan things like you were little in the grocery store. Hope you like it, and if you don't, let me know how I can improve. I've always been a fan of continuous improvement. God I hope this is as cool as I'm hoping.

So what is this> 

Simple:

I want it to take thes tuff I buy, keep track of it without the copying and pasting and upkeeep, tell me how much I spent, where I've stored the dang thing, how much of it I have, what I can make with it (or use it for) and how to make it - along with some help with troubleshooting. Holy cow did I have this underestimated, but I have to do it.
